What this view can tell you

Each point is one real iNaturalist photo represented by the encoder named in the cache provenance below. Nearby photos in the image representation can share visible traits, backgrounds, poses or photographic style. They are candidates for comparison, not species identifications. Cosine similarity uses all embedding dimensions. The two-dimensional PCA plot discards information, so its apparent distances can disagree with the closest-match list.

A rectangular brush selects photos in the lossy PCA display only. It is an exploratory set, not a biological class or a full-space similarity boundary. The same set is outlined in teal on the geographic map. Use Add selected photo and Remove selected photo to build or edit the same set with the keyboard, using search and the observation selector. The optional brushed-photo filter intersects with map-view and search filters. Changing life group clears the brush.

Representation colors encode three sign-fixed PCA components as red, green and blue. Channels are independently stretched between their 2nd and 98th percentiles and clipped; missing or constant axes use neutral gray channels. Color fits are separate for every life group and encoder, so hues cannot be compared across groups or models. They describe a lossy photo representation, not habitat or species classes.

Cross-encoder overlap compares stored neighbor ID sets from complete encoder pipelines, including their preprocessing. The same observation IDs do not prove identical source image bytes. It does not recompute rankings from rounded published scores.

Life groups use separate projections and, when supplied, separate reference samples. Colors mark selection, not species or habitat. Distances and scores are not calibrated across groups. Sampling does not measure local abundance, ecological absence or a site's unsurveyed biodiversity. Coordinates come from the public observation pool and can be obscured by iNaturalist; no private coordinate fields are used.

When a reference sample is included, distance is 1 minus the largest cosine similarity to any reference photo in the same life group. A higher value means less visually familiar to this particular sample. It is not species novelty, identification confidence or a probability. The frozen reference is incomplete, and its labels are recorded identifications, not independent verification. Some candidate observation IDs also occur in the reference; their own photo can give distance zero. Those observations are marked below.
